Beginner's guide

FTTH Design Studio in plain English

The problem it solves

To bring fiber-optic internet to a neighborhood (FTTH, fiber to the home), engineers first design the network: where every cable goes, how big each cable is, where the equipment sits and what it all costs. By hand, that takes days or weeks per neighborhood. This tool does it automatically, in about a second.

How it works

  1. Upload home addresses. Optionally add a street map, the area outline and the main equipment location.
  2. The tool designs the network, connecting every home with the least cable and cost.
  3. Review the results: a design map, a cost estimate and equipment lists to download.

Key terms

OLT / central officeThe "headquarters" building where the internet signal starts.
SplitterSplits one fiber signal so it can serve many homes, like a power strip for internet.
1:8 → 1:8The signal splits into 8, then each of those into 8 again, so one signal serves up to 64 homes.
MST (terminal)A small box near the homes that connects up to 8 houses.
DA (distribution area)A zone of up to 250 homes served by the same group of splitters.
F1 / F2 cableF1 is the main "highway" cable from the headquarters; F2 is the smaller "neighborhood street" cable.
DropThe short cable from the street into each house.
Handhole / flowerpotUnderground boxes where cables are joined or reached.
Aerial vs. undergroundCable hung on utility poles (cheaper) or buried (more expensive, especially in rock).
MDUApartment buildings. They are flagged and set aside because they need a different design.

What you see on screen

  • Left panel: your settings in numbered steps: upload data, design style, poles, costs and map display.
  • Map: the finished design on satellite imagery: colored lines are cables, symbols are equipment, outlined zones are areas.
  • Tabs below: Overview compares design options, Design check confirms every rule is met, Cost has the full price breakdown, Network schedules lists the equipment, Export downloads everything.

Why it matters

  • Speed: seconds instead of days of manual drafting.
  • Cost answers: cost per home right away, checked against your budget (default $2,200 per home).
  • Compares options: tries several design styles and highlights the cheapest one that meets every rule.
  • Real-world details: rock, permits, utility poles and apartment buildings.
Try it: the tool opens on a finished sample for 603 homes in Quail Creek, North Austin, Texas. Click around the map and the tabs, then upload your own addresses to design a new area. Good to know: prices are sample numbers, so replace them with local quotes before bidding, and final construction drawings still need an engineer's review.
